# Methods, data and references

SI units throughout except where a field's convention is followed (Antoine constants in mmHg and degC,
permeabilities in Barrer in the data table, solubilities in g per 100 g water).

## Vapour-liquid equilibrium and flash (`vle`, `flash`)

Antoine constants from Lange's Handbook of Chemistry (Dean, 15th ed.), Table 5.29, validated against NIST
normal boiling points; Raoult's law and the modified form with activity coefficients from the Wilson equation
(Wilson 1964), with binary parameters regressed to 1 atm data as tabulated in Perry's Chemical Engineers'
Handbook and in Seader, Henley & Roper (indicative, within 1-2 % on azeotrope compositions); relative
volatility and its geometric-mean estimate; azeotropes by locating the crossing of the x-y curve with the
diagonal. Rachford & Rice (1952) for the isothermal flash; bubble and dew points by their defining sums; the
adiabatic flash by an enthalpy balance with constant liquid heat capacities and latent heats.

## Distillation (`distillation`)

McCabe & Thiele (1925) with the q-line of feed thermal condition; minimum reflux from the pinch with a check
for a tangent pinch; Fenske (1932), Underwood (1948), Gilliland (1940) in Molokanov's (1972) analytic form,
Kirkbride (1944); the Rayleigh equation and batch rectification at constant reflux by stage stepping;
O'Connell's (1946) efficiency correlation. Presentation follows Seader, Henley & Roper, *Separation Process
Principles* (4th ed.), Ch. 7 and 9, and McCabe, Smith & Harriott, *Unit Operations of Chemical Engineering*
(7th ed.), Ch. 21.

## Absorption, packed towers and mass transfer (`absorption`, `transport`)

Kremser (1930) and Souders & Brown (1932); the transfer-unit method of Chilton & Colburn (1935) and Colburn's
(1939) closed form; the two-film theory of Whitman (1923); Fuller, Schettler & Giddings (1966) and Wilke &
Chang (1955) diffusivities; Stefan diffusion; Higbie's (1935) penetration theory; the Frossling and
Linton-Sherwood correlations; the Chilton-Colburn analogy. Seader Ch. 3 and 6; Treybal, *Mass-Transfer
Operations* (3rd ed.).

## Extraction and leaching (`extraction`)

The Kremser form for immiscible solvents; triangular (right-triangle) coordinates, tie-line interpolation, the
lever rule and the Hunter-Nash (1934) construction with the operating point, and minimum solvent by
bisection on the stage count; countercurrent leaching with constant underflow (Seader Ch. 8 and 16; Treybal
Ch. 10 and 13). The acetic acid - water - isopropyl ether tie lines follow Treybal / Seader Example 8.1.

## Adsorption, ion exchange and chromatography (`adsorption`)

Langmuir (1918), Freundlich and BET (1938) isotherms; the ideal (shock) wave; the Bohart-Adams (1920) /
Thomas constant-pattern model; Klinkenberg's (1948) approximation for linear isotherms; the length of unused
bed of Collins (1967) as presented in Seader Ch. 15 and Wankat, *Separation Process Engineering*, Ch. 18;
a pressure-swing working-capacity balance. The chromatography plate model (Martin & Synge 1941) and van
Deemter (1956) are used in the notebooks.

## Membranes (`membranes`)

Solution-diffusion permeation (Wijmans & Baker 1995); the complete-mixing (Weller & Steiner 1950) and
cross-flow models for a binary gas; van 't Hoff osmotic pressure; the solution-diffusion model of reverse
osmosis (Lonsdale, Merten & Riley 1965) with water and salt permeabilities; film-theory concentration
polarisation; the gel-polarisation model of ultrafiltration; countercurrent dialysis by the heat-exchanger
analogy. Baker, *Membrane Technology and Applications* (3rd ed.); Seader Ch. 14. Permeabilities in the data
table are indicative values from Baker Table 8.1 and Robeson (2008).

## Crystallization (`crystallization`)

Solubilities from the CRC Handbook of Chemistry and Physics; yields by solute and solvent balances including
water of crystallisation; the MSMPR population balance of Randolph & Larson, *Theory of Particulate
Processes* (2nd ed., 1988): exponential population density, moments, dominant size 3 G tau, kinetics from a
semi-log plot; Mullin, *Crystallization* (4th ed.), for supersaturation and the metastable zone.

## Drying, evaporation and humidification (`drying_evaporation`)

Psychrometry from the Antoine vapour pressure of water (humidity, dew point, adiabatic saturation, the Lewis
relation for wet-bulb); drying-rate curves with constant and linear falling-rate periods (McCabe, Smith &
Harriott Ch. 24); single- and multiple-effect evaporators with equal areas by iteration on the temperature
drops (McCabe Ch. 16; Seader); Merkel's (1925) enthalpy driving force for cooling towers in the notebooks.

## Data (`datasets`)

Ethanol-water VLE: Carey & Lewis (1932) as in Perry's Table 13-1. Permeabilities: Baker; Robeson. Solubility:
CRC. Tie lines: Treybal / Seader. All pseudo-experimental files are generated by `tools/make_course_data.py`
from the library's own models with the parameters, noise and deliberate faults stated in their headers.

## General references

Seader, Henley & Roper, *Separation Process Principles*, 4th ed. (2016) · Wankat, *Separation Process
Engineering*, 4th ed. (2017) · McCabe, Smith & Harriott, *Unit Operations of Chemical Engineering*, 7th ed.
(2005) · Treybal, *Mass-Transfer Operations*, 3rd ed. (1980) · Baker, *Membrane Technology and Applications*,
3rd ed. (2012) · Mullin, *Crystallization*, 4th ed. (2001).
